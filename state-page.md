## MEDICAID ENROLLMENT SECTION  (TEMPLATED)

SECTION_ID: medicaid-enrollment
H2: Medicaid Enrollment
INTRO: Alabama Medicaid is administered by the Alabama Medicaid Agency, a state agency that operates independently of the federal Medicaid framework while following CMS guidelines. There is no single national portal. Providers must complete enrollment directly through the Alabama Medicaid Agency before they can bill for services rendered to Medicaid recipients in the state. Federal law requires that physicians and other practitioners who prescribe, order, refer, or bill services for Alabama Medicaid recipients be enrolled as Medicaid providers. This applies to both individual providers and group practices.

SUB_1_H3: How Alabama Medicaid Provider Enrollment Works
SUB_1_BODY: When a provider enrolls with the Alabama Medicaid Agency, they are registering at the fee-for-service level. This is the foundational enrollment step. After state fee-for-service enrollment is confirmed, providers who want to participate with Alabama Medicaid managed care organizations (MCOs) must complete a separate credentialing and enrollment process with each MCO individually. These are two distinct processes, and MCOs will not begin their review until the state enrollment is confirmed.

SUB_2_H3: Enrollment Type Selection
SUB_2_BODY: Choosing the wrong enrollment type is the most common mistake Alabama providers make when attempting to enroll on their own. The Alabama Medicaid Agency requires providers to select the correct enrollment category based on provider type, specialty, and billing structure. An incorrect selection results in a denial that requires starting the process over. Our team confirms the correct enrollment type before any application is submitted.

SUB_3_H3: CAQH ProView and Alabama Medicaid
SUB_3_BODY: The Alabama Medicaid Agency interfaces with CAQH ProView as a primary source for provider data. An expired CAQH attestation, which requires re-attestation every 120 days, can trigger rejection of a pending Alabama Medicaid application. Contracting Providers monitors attestation schedules for every provider we work with to prevent this from happening.

SUB_4_H3: Revalidation Requirements
SUB_4_BODY: Federal law requires Alabama Medicaid providers to revalidate their enrollment periodically. Missing a revalidation deadline results in automatic disenrollment. The provider can no longer bill Medicaid until re-enrollment is complete. Our team tracks revalidation dates and initiates the process in advance of deadlines to prevent any gap in enrollment status.

SUB_5_H3: Payer-Specific Nuances

PAYER_1_H4: Alabama Medicaid Agency (Fee-for-Service)
PAYER_1_BODY: Enrollment is completed through the Alabama Medicaid Agency provider enrollment portal. Providers must select the correct enrollment type before submission. An incorrect selection results in a denial and requires restarting the process.

PAYER_2_H4: MCO Enrollment in Alabama
PAYER_2_BODY: After state Medicaid fee-for-service enrollment is confirmed, providers who want to bill through Alabama Medicaid managed care plans must enroll separately with each MCO operating in the state. These MCO enrollments run on independent timelines and involve their own credentialing processes. Contracting Providers coordinates MCO enrollment following state approval so providers achieve full billing access as efficiently as possible.

PAYER_3_H4: NPI and Taxonomy Requirements
PAYER_3_BODY: Every Alabama Medicaid application requires a valid National Provider Identifier. For group practices, a Type 2 NPI is required in addition to each rendering provider's Type 1. Taxonomy codes must accurately reflect the provider's specialty. Mismatched taxonomy codes are a common cause of rejections and can set a provider back four to six weeks.

PAYER_4_H4: Disclaimer
PAYER_4_BODY: Contracting Providers is not the Alabama Medicaid Agency. We help healthcare providers prepare, manage, and complete payer enrollment and credentialing tasks, including Alabama Medicaid provider enrollment support. The Alabama Medicaid Agency controls all final enrollment decisions and approvals.

## PROVIDER CREDENTIALING SECTION  (TEMPLATED)

SECTION_ID: provider-credentialing
H2: Provider Credentialing

PARAGRAPH_1: Alabama Medicaid provider enrollment and provider credentialing are closely linked but distinct processes. Enrollment grants a provider the right to bill the Alabama Medicaid Agency for fee-for-service claims. Credentialing verifies that the provider meets the clinical and professional standards required by the Alabama Medicaid program and its managed care organizations.
PARAGRAPH_2: Most Alabama Medicaid MCOs require credentialing as a condition of in-network participation, even after state enrollment is complete. Without credentialing, a provider may be enrolled at the state level but unable to bill through the managed care organizations that serve the majority of Alabama Medicaid beneficiaries.

INVOLVES_H3: What Credentialing Involves
INVOLVES_ITEM_1: Primary source verification of Alabama medical licenses, DEA certificates, and board certifications
INVOLVES_ITEM_2: Review of malpractice history and National Practitioner Data Bank queries
INVOLVES_ITEM_3: Hospital affiliation verification where applicable
INVOLVES_ITEM_4: CAQH ProView data review and completion
INVOLVES_ITEM_5: MCO-specific credentialing committee review and approval

TIMELINES_H3: Credentialing Timelines
TIMELINES_BODY: Credentialing through an Alabama Medicaid MCO typically takes 60 to 120 days from initial application submission. Delays occur most commonly due to incomplete applications, outdated CAQH data, or unresolved primary source verification issues. Contracting Providers front-loads the process with a pre-submission audit to minimize avoidable delays.

BILLING_READINESS_H3: Credentialing and Billing Readiness
BILLING_READINESS_BODY: A provider who completes state enrollment but has not yet been credentialed by the relevant Alabama MCOs may face a gap period during which claims are denied. Our team coordinates enrollment and credentialing timelines simultaneously to reduce or eliminate that gap.
